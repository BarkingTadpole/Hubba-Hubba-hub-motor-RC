Wheel angle calibration

Left: uncalibrated · Right: uncalibrated

  1. Disconnect traction power; power the servos. Set toe to 0°, center CH1/CH2, and keep calibrated CH4 at STOP. Adjust each servo trim until its wheel points straight ahead.
  2. Select a wheel and start measurement mode. CH1 moves that wheel within its saved PWM/linkage limits; the other stays at trimmed neutral. Hold each position still, capture its PWM, then enter the measured road-wheel angle.
  3. Record straight ahead (0°), left (negative) and right (positive). Add intermediate measurements for nonlinear linkage motion, up to 9 points. Finish measurement mode, review the changes, then save. Repeat for the other wheel.

PWM is the commanded servo position. Measure the wheel relative to the vehicle’s straight-ahead direction, not the servo horn. Captures do not measure angles. Mode ends on receiver loss, CH4 leaving STOP, throttle movement or 120 seconds without a capture.

Drive & receiver

Tank turning

Select Tank in Drivetrain while disarmed. CH1 selects left or right rotation; either direction of CH2 controls power. Center CH1 or CH2 to stop. Center both before arming.

Default 25%. Limits throttle equally on both sides, independently of the reverse limit; 0% disables tank power. This is a throttle cap, not a measured speed target.

Left turn: left motors reverse, right motors forward. Right turn: the opposite. Front wheels hold the configured toe position; Ackermann is bypassed. Both sides stop before reversing.

Rear RPM conversion

Steering input

CH1 · µs

Center must sit ≥150 µs between endpoints; deadband must be smaller than each span. Or use Calibrate → CH1 Steering.

Tank mode keeps the median filter and bypasses steering smoothing so an old spin direction cannot linger.

Vehicle geometry

Steering limits must fit both servos' calibrated angles. Lower limits before reducing endpoint angles. Wheel diameter also sets the rear-speed conversion.

Toe alignment

One shared road-wheel angle at centered CH1. Positive = toe-in (fronts toward each other); negative = toe-out. Default 0°.

Available range comes from both wheels’ saved calibration, trims and linkage limits, in 0.1° steps.

Toe is added after steering geometry and counts toward linkage travel. Calibrated endpoints and neutral trims stay fixed. Startup, input loss and tank mode hold this alignment.

Safe linkage travel

Maximum servo rotation from trimmed straight-ahead. Defaults: 90° inside and 68° outside. Both values apply to either wheel according to the turn.

Inside is the wheel toward the turn: left wheel in a left turn, right wheel in a right turn. The other wheel is outside.

Range 0–90° in 0.1° steps, including toe travel. Tighter calibrated endpoints still apply. Steering components reduce together to preserve their Ackermann blend. Inside travel bounds toe-out; outside travel bounds toe-in. Road-wheel calibration angles remain separate.

Servo calibration

PWM & wheel angle

180° positional servo · 500–2500 µs · nominal neutral 1500 µs · output 50 Hz. Endpoints must stay ≥50 µs either side of base center and trimmed neutral.

Neutral = base center + trim. Positive trim increases PWM; endpoints stay fixed. Set trim to 0 to reset.

Angles below are measured road-wheel travel (1–85°), not the servo's 180° shaft range. Default 30° values need measurement; initial PWM travel is 1000–2000 µs.

Use Calibrate → Wheel angle calibration to replace the endpoint approximation with measured points. Saved measurements lock that wheel’s trim and endpoints until cleared.

180° servo specs & mounting

Rated input: 50–330 Hz; internal deadband: 2 µs. This is separate from the CH1 receiver deadband. Increasing PWM rotates counterclockwise (CCW); decreasing PWM rotates clockwise (CW).

Ackermann strength versus speed

0% = parallel · 100% = ideal Ackermann, before toe. Inside = wheel toward the turn; it follows steering input within linkage limits. Strength blends the outside angle toward the ideal geometry. Tank mode bypasses this curve.

Interpolation & speed loss

Linear interpolation; final percentage holds above the last speed. Applies in reverse too. Armed RPM loss holds the last valid speed, including a stop with no pulses. Before a valid estimate, or when disarmed with missing RPM, the 0 km/h point applies.
